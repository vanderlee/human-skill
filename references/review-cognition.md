# Additional cognitive considerations for human review

Research reviewed 15 October 2026. These candidates were reviewed by the user and added to the skill. They are task-dependent tendencies and effects, not a personality taxonomy, individual diagnosis, or claim that every reviewer behaves the same way.

The practical rules in SKILL.md apply proportionately within the requested scope. The applications below are engineering inferences unless a study directly investigates the stated programming/review behavior. Supporting evidence for related existing guidance remains in [cognition-evidence.md](cognition-evidence.md).

## Aesthetics and perception

| Concern | Evidence and limitation | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Processing fluency and aesthetic preference | [Reber, Schwarz and Winkielman (2004)](https://psy2.ucsd.edu/~pwinkiel/reber-schwarz-winkielman-beauty-PSPR-2004.pdf) connect processing fluency with aesthetic pleasure in a theoretical synthesis. This does not establish that a pleasing code layout is correct. | Favor consistent, recognizable presentation that reveals actual structure; check behavior separately from aesthetic judgments. |
| Aesthetic halo | [Tractinsky, Katz and Ikar (2000)](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/what-is-beautiful-is-usable-2/) found aesthetics influenced perceived usability in an ATM-like interface experiment. Applying this concern to polished code or AI explanations is an extrapolation. | Treat visual polish and fluent prose as presentation, not proof. A professional-looking solution still needs contract-based evidence. |
| Visual structure and control-flow comprehension | [Morzeck et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.5220/0012087500003538) studied 20 students/professionals answering Java control-flow questions and found indentation improved response time. The task does not establish a universal optimal indentation width or method size. | Align visual nesting and grouping with behavior and the project formatter. Do not impose aesthetic symmetry on operations with different contracts. |

## Persistent assumptions and intentions: cognitive ghosts

Cognitive ghosts is an informal umbrella here. Use the specific mechanisms below when making research claims; do not present it as a clinical term or one established cognitive construct.

| Concern | Evidence and limitation | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Continued influence of corrected information | [Rich, Donovan and Rapp (2023)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36201841/) investigated updating after causal explanations were corrected; initial information can continue influencing reasoning. These are narrative-reasoning studies, not refactoring trials. | Explain the replacement for a superseded contract explicitly. Update misleading active material while keeping historical records clearly historical. |
| Mental set / Einstellung | [Sergeyuk et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06673) investigated familiar solutions constraining programming problem solving. Their tested editor-color and forget-previous-solutions interventions were insufficient to overcome the effect. | Verify that a familiar design pattern fits the present problem; consider a simpler alternative. Do not claim that a theme change or an instruction to forget reliably removes bias. |
| Completed-intention aftereffects | [Strengthening Encoding via Implementation Intention Formation Increases Prospective Memory Commission Errors (2013)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3669231/) found that no-longer-required intentions could still produce responses in a laboratory task. Applying this to developer plans is a lower-confidence extrapolation. | Keep pending, completed, and cancelled work distinct at handoff; do not carry abandoned plan steps forward as current requirements. This supports clear task state, not a new mandatory workflow. |

## Reasoning, attention, and review

| Concern | Evidence and limitation | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Illusion of explanatory depth | [Rozenblit and Keil (2002)](https://cogdevlab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/rozenblit%20%26%20keil%20%202002.pdf) showed gaps between perceived understanding and detailed explanatory ability for everyday mechanisms. Transfer to code review is an inference. | Trace actual inputs, branches, state, effects, outputs, and failures. Do not accept a plausible architectural summary as complete implementation understanding. |
| Automation overreliance and misplaced confidence | [Perry et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03622) found security and confidence problems under a particular code-assistant model and task set. [Bucinca et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692) reduced overreliance through cognitive forcing in a different decision-support setting, with lower subjective ratings for more effective interventions. | Separate generated explanations from checked evidence. Use independent contract checks for consequential decisions without imposing ritual friction on every small edit. Do not generalize effect sizes to current assistants. |
| Subtraction neglect | [Adams et al. (2021)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03380-y) found beneficial subtractive transformations were often overlooked across eight general transformation experiments. These were not software maintenance tasks. | Consider removing or consolidating mechanisms before adding another layer, while preserving necessary behavior, snapshots, caches, and lifecycle distinctions. |
| File-position effects | [Fregnan et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04259) combined repository analysis with a 106-participant experiment: position affected detection of one seeded defect type, not the other. | Provide a meaningful reading route and highlight consequential later files. Do not assume alphabetical order distributes attention evenly or invent a universally optimal ordering. |
| Rare-target detection limitations | [Wolfe, Horowitz and Kenner (2005)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4224304/) observed more misses at low target prevalence in artificial visual screening tasks. Code-review transfer and useful interventions require evaluation. | For relevant high-impact exceptional behavior, identify targeted checks instead of relying solely on scanning. This is supporting rationale, not a justification for importing miss rates or auditing every conceivable risk. |
| Habituation to repeated warnings | [Anderson et al. (2016)](https://jmis-web.org/articles/1304) studied habituation to security warnings using neurophysiological and behavioral experiments. Their changing-warning artifact is not evidence for randomizing source-code presentation. | Keep findings and warnings specific, prioritized, and actionable; avoid repetitive generic cautions. Preserve consistent code formatting. |
| Information scent and navigation | [Lawrance et al. (2008)](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~burnett/Reprints/chi08-infoForage.pdf) modeled programmers' navigation during maintenance using linguistic cues and code topology. This supports navigation reasoning, not a guarantee of correctness from naming alone. | Make requirement vocabulary, names, module ownership, and test links guide reviewers to relevant behavior; avoid wrappers that hide its location. |
| Attention guided by existing review comments | [Spadini, Calikli and Bacchelli (2020)](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/258206/) studied 85 developers. Existing comments could help detect another instance of an otherwise overlooked bug type; the findings do not establish that prior comments are universally harmful. | Treat findings as useful search cues, verify each occurrence, and cover relevant areas beyond the existing discussion. Do not mandate hiding comments or equate previous approval with complete review. |

## Evaluation and limits

Prefer observable review outcomes: whether the intended maintainer understands the changed contract, finds relevant implementation and evidence, and identifies a known problem with less reconstruction work. Assess preference, response time, confidence, and defect detection separately. No single metric proves overall review quality.

Use these considerations to strengthen existing scope, naming, grouping, review-route, and evidence rules rather than generating fourteen checklist items for every task. Do not infer a reader's medical condition, prescribe fixed attention budgets, or use the research to justify changing program contracts. Completed-intention and rare-target applications remain supporting rationale rather than claims of experimentally validated code-review interventions.
